Why it works

Two things your body makes, and the foods it makes them from

You do not swallow DIM or glutathione. Your body makes both of them, out of food you put on your plate.

Cruciferous→Glucosinolates→DIM

Broccoli, cabbage and kale become DIM

Chewing and digestion break the glucosinolates in cruciferous vegetables down into indole-3-carbinol, and your stomach converts a share of that into DIM. You eat the vegetable. Your body makes the DIM.

Sulfur foods→Cysteine→Glutathione

Eggs, garlic and onions become glutathione

Your body builds glutathione from three amino acids, and cysteine is the bottleneck. Cysteine rides in on sulfur-rich foods, which is why eggs, garlic, onions and the cruciferous family keep showing up on the same list.
