Before you start

Why 15 minutes can change your whole day

You're probably the one who keeps everyone else's day running. You make the breakfasts, keep track of the schedules, answer the texts and remember the permission slips. Your own health keeps getting pushed to tomorrow. It's not that you don't care. There's just never a minute left over.

Here's the trouble with that: when you run on empty, everyone gets less of you. You have less patience, you're foggy by mid-morning, and by night you're too tired to enjoy the people you're doing it all for. Taking care of yourself doesn't take anything away from your family. It's what lets you keep showing up for them.

That's what this guide is for: 15 minutes each morning that put your health first, so you can put your family first.

Why the morning?

How your morning goes tends to set the tone for the rest of your day. Start out rushed, hungry and answering your phone, and you spend the day catching up. Start out fed, with a plan and a few calm minutes, and you have more to give when the day gets messy. You can't control what the day throws at you, but you can control the first 15 minutes.

Why only 15 minutes?

Most morning routines ask for an hour and a 5 AM alarm. They last about a week, because real life doesn't have an hour to spare. Fifteen minutes fits even on a busy morning, and a small routine you keep will do more for you than a big one you quit.

Why three parts?

Feeling good comes down to three things, and each five minutes covers one of them. Skip one, and you'll feel it by 10 AM.

  1. 5:00Fuel yourself  takes care of your body. Water and a real breakfast.
  2. 5:00Get focused  takes care of your head. A short plan for your day.
  3. 5:00Fill your cup  takes care of your heart. A few minutes that are just yours.

Why it sticks.

Every new habit in this guide is tied to something you already do without thinking, like turning off your alarm or pouring your coffee. The old habit becomes your reminder for the new one, so you don't have to rely on willpower to remember. And you add them one at a time, so nothing has to change overnight.

Start here

How to use this guide

  • Find your 15 minutes. Pick where they fit: before the house wakes up, while the kids eat, or right after everyone's out the door. You don't have to get up at 5.
  • Each week adds one five-minute part. That's three habits a week. Add one new habit every two or three days, and keep doing the ones before it.
  • Each habit comes with a sentence to say to yourself. Say it once, out loud if you can. Tomorrow, the alarm will remind you.

    After I turn off my alarm, I'll drink a full glass of water.

  • Write one line a day on the check-in page. Write your energy, your mood, and whether you did your 15. It takes about 30 seconds.
  • Missed a morning? Pick it back up the next day. One missed day doesn't undo anything.
  • A habit keeps slipping? It isn't you. Tie it to a different part of your morning.
  • Rough morning? Do the 5-minute version at the back of this guide instead.
Week one · 5 minutes

Fuel yourself

The first five minutes are about your body. A lot of busy moms start the day on coffee alone and wonder why they crash by 10. These three habits put something good in first, in the order you'd do them: water when you wake up, a breakfast with protein, then your coffee, sitting down.

Habit 1

Drink a glass of water first thing

What it is
A full glass of water, before your coffee and before anything else.
Why
You go seven or eight hours overnight without a drink, and you lose water just breathing, so you wake up a little low. That can make you tired, foggy or headachy, and it's easy to mistake it for needing more coffee. Water first tops you back up.
How
Before bed, fill a glass and leave it by the sink or your bed. When you turn off your alarm, drink the whole glass.
Say to yourself

After I turn off my alarm, I'll drink a full glass of water before I do anything else.

Habit 2

Eat a breakfast with protein in it

What it is
A breakfast built around protein: eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, chicken, or a protein shake. Not toast, cereal or a granola bar on its own.
Why
Your body takes longer to digest protein, so it keeps you full for hours. A breakfast that's mostly carbs gives you quick energy that drops off fast. That's the mid-morning slump, when you're suddenly starving and snacking. Aim for about 25 to 30 grams. A cup of Greek yogurt gets you most of the way.
How
When you make breakfast for everyone else, make a little extra protein for you. Then sit down and eat it.
Say to yourself

After I make everyone else's breakfast, I'll make mine and sit down to eat it.

Habit 3

Drink your coffee sitting down

What it is
The first few sips of your coffee or tea, in a chair, before you start the day's chores.
Why
Most moms drink their coffee on the move and barely taste it. A few minutes sitting still gives your mind a pause before everyone needs something from you. The chair you pick this week is also where week two happens.
How
Pick one chair as your morning spot. Pour your coffee, sit there, and take your first few sips before you clear the counter.
Say to yourself

After I pour my coffee, I'll sit down for the first few sips.

Week two · 5 minutes

Get focused

The next five minutes are about your head. Instead of starting the day on your phone with everyone else's needs, you sit in your morning chair with your coffee and decide what today is about. Three habits: write a short list, make sure you're on it, and keep your phone away until it's done.

Habit 4

Write down the 3 most important things for today

What it is
A short list, on paper, of the three things you most want to get done today. It's not your whole to-do list, just the three that matter most. This guide calls it your "top 3."
Why
A list of 20 things never gets finished, so at bedtime all you see is what didn't get done, even on a day you worked hard. Choosing three makes you decide what matters. Writing them down gets them out of your head, so you stop juggling them all day. Finish three, and you go to bed feeling like you got somewhere.
How
Keep a small notebook and pen next to your morning chair. When you sit down with your coffee, write three things for today.
Say to yourself

After I sit down with my coffee, I'll write my top 3 for today.

Habit 5

Make one of the three about you

What it is
One of your three is something for your own health or happiness: a walk, a call with a friend, a real lunch, or bed by 10.
Why
When you look after everyone else, your own needs fall to the bottom, and what isn't written down usually doesn't happen. On the list, it's a plan instead of a wish. Taking care of you is what keeps you going for them.
How
After you write your three, read them back. If none of them is for you, cross off the smallest one and write in something that is.
Say to yourself

After I write my top 3, I'll check that one of them is for me.

Habit 6

Keep your phone face down until your list is written

What it is
No texts, email or social media until your three are on paper. It's about five minutes.
Why
The minute you open your phone, everyone else's needs pour in, and your brain switches to reacting. Writing your own list first means your day starts with what you decided matters. Then you answer everyone else. Your messages will wait five minutes.
How
When you pick up your phone from the charger, put it face down, or leave it on the charger in the kitchen, until your list is written.
Say to yourself

After I pick up my phone from the charger, I'll put it face down until my top 3 is written.

Week three · 5 minutes

Fill your cup

The last five minutes are just for you. "Fill your cup" means doing something that gives you energy instead of using it up. It's the part most people skip, and it matters most for how you feel. Three small things: five quiet minutes, a minute of daylight, and one easy stretch.

Habit 7

Take five minutes just for you

What it is
Five minutes doing one thing that calms you: pray, stretch, play a song you love, read a page, or sit in the quiet.
Why
Your day is full of other people's needs. A few calm minutes first help you start steady instead of frazzled, and that's what you bring to your family. Doing the same thing all week means you don't waste the time choosing.
How
Pick one thing. After you write your top 3, do it for five minutes.
Say to yourself

After I finish my top 3, I'll take five minutes for me.

Habit 8

Get a minute of daylight

What it is
One minute outside, or in an open doorway, looking at the sky.
Why
Your body has an inner clock that tells you when to feel awake and when to feel sleepy. Morning daylight is the main thing that sets it each day. Even on a cloudy day, it's much brighter outside than under your kitchen lights. A minute of it helps you feel more awake now and sleepier at bedtime.
How
When you open the curtains, step outside for one minute with your phone in your pocket. If it's cold or raining, stand at the window.
Say to yourself

After I open the curtains, I'll step outside or stand at the door for one minute.

Habit 9

Do one easy stretch

What it is
Three simple moves that take about a minute: reach up, fold forward, roll your shoulders.
Why
After a night lying still, your muscles are stiff, especially your neck, shoulders and back, where a lot of us carry stress. A gentle stretch gets your blood moving and loosens you up for the day.
How
After you brush your teeth, reach up toward the ceiling for three slow breaths. Fold forward with soft knees for three more. Then roll your shoulders back five times.
Say to yourself

After I brush my teeth, I'll reach up tall, fold forward and roll my shoulders.

Week four · 15 minutes

Make it yours

You've now tried all nine habits. This week you put the three parts together into one 15-minute morning, look at which habits gave you the most energy, and pick the ones you'll keep.

Habit 10

Do the whole 15 in the order that fits your house

What it is
All three parts back to back, about 15 minutes: fuel, focus and fill.
Why
There's no right order. What counts is doing it most mornings, so the order should fit when your house is quiet.
How
If you're up before the kids, go fuel, focus, fill. If your quiet time comes after drop-off, go fill, fuel, focus.
Say to yourself

After I turn off my alarm, I'll start my 15.

Habit 11

Look back at your week

What it is
On Sunday, reading back the one-line notes you wrote each day on the check-in page: your energy, your mood, and whether you did your 15.
Why
Everyone's different. Your notes show you patterns you'd never notice otherwise. Maybe your best days were the ones you ate eggs, or the ones you got outside.
How
On Sunday, with your coffee, read the week. Find the days your energy was best and look at what you did those mornings. Circle anything that shows up more than once.
Say to yourself

After I pour my Sunday coffee, I'll read back the week's check-ins.

Habit 12

Keep the habits that worked

What it is
On day 30, choosing the habits you'll keep.
Why
The habits that fit your life are the ones that last. Drop the rest without guilt.
How
Go through all nine. Keep the ones that fit your mornings, write them on the fridge page, and put it on your fridge.
Say to yourself

On day 30, I'll pick the habits I'm keeping.

For the hard mornings

The 5-minute version

For the mornings that fall apart, like a sick kid, an early meeting, or an alarm that didn't go off, do just these three:

  • Drink a glass of water
  • Write your top 3 on anything you can find
  • Take three slow breaths before you walk out the door
