Clean Iced Coffee

8 refreshing iced coffees you can pack for the beach, the pool, or the trail. No syrup bombs, no sugar crash.

Sweetened only with honey, agave, and stevia. Real ingredients, real energy.

The Problem With Summer Iced Coffee

A drive-thru iced coffee can carry forty grams of sugar before you leave the lot. You get the lift, then the crash, and the crash hits hardest on a hot day when you are already low on water.

The coffee was never the problem. The syrups, sweetened condensed milk, and flavored creamers are. Cut those, sweeten with a little honey, agave, or stevia, and you keep everything worth having: cold, creamy, and light enough to drink all afternoon.

All 8 recipes pack into a thermos and hold on ice. Most take two minutes. Recipe 6 is the fastest of all: one coffee stick, water, then ice. It is the one you actually throw in a beach bag.

Two things that keep it clean:

  1. Skip the flavored creamers. That is where the hidden sugar lives. Use real or unsweetened plant milk.
  2. Chase the caffeine with water. Heat and coffee both pull it out of you.
On the numbers: the calorie and sugar figures are estimates for planning, not lab values. They move with your milk and your sweetener.

How to read each recipe:

  • Each one makes one tall glass by default. Want a batch for the cooler? Scale the ingredients up and mix it in a pitcher the night before.
  • Sweetener amounts are a starting point. Taste and adjust. Cold drinks read as less sweet than warm ones.
  • Every recipe notes whether it is thermos friendly so you know what travels.

THE 8 RECIPES

Recipe 01

Cold Brew, Coconut Milk And Honey

Why it works: the clean classic. Cold brew is naturally smooth and low in acid, coconut milk makes it creamy without a drop of sugar, and a little honey rounds it off. Three ingredients, zero junk.

Approx. per glass: 90 cal · 8g sugar (from the honey)

Thermos friendly: yes

IngredientAmount
Cold brew coffee1 cup
Unsweetened coconut milk (carton, not canned)¼ cup
Honey1 tsp
Ice1 cup

Method:

  1. Stir the honey into the cold brew first, while there is no ice to fight it, until it fully dissolves.
  2. Fill a glass or thermos with ice.
  3. Pour the sweetened cold brew over the top.
  4. Finish with the coconut milk and give it one gentle stir.

Tipmake the cold brew in a jar overnight (½ cup coarse grounds to 4 cups water, strain in the morning) and you have four days of clean coffee ready to go.

Recipe 02

Vanilla Almond Iced Latte

Why it works: unsweetened almond milk keeps it light, real vanilla brings the sweetness your brain expects, and a touch of agave finishes it. It tastes like a coffee-shop vanilla latte without the syrup pump.

Approx. per glass: 70 cal · 5g sugar

Thermos friendly: yes

IngredientAmount
Strong brewed coffee or espresso, chilled¾ cup
Unsweetened almond milk½ cup
Pure vanilla extract¼ tsp
Agave nectar1 tsp
Ice1 cup

Method:

  1. Stir the vanilla and agave into the chilled coffee until smooth.
  2. Fill your glass with ice.
  3. Pour the coffee over, then top with almond milk.
  4. Stir once and serve.

Tipfreeze leftover coffee in an ice cube tray and use those instead of water ice. Your latte stays strong instead of watering down in the sun.

Recipe 03

Cinnamon Cold Brew

Why it works: cinnamon tricks the palate into reading a drink as sweet, so you need barely any added sweetener. Oat milk gives it body, agave ties it together, and cinnamon has its own gentle blood-sugar friendly reputation.

Approx. per glass: 80 cal · 6g sugar

Thermos friendly: yes

IngredientAmount
Cold brew coffee1 cup
Unsweetened oat milk¼ cup
Ground cinnamon¼ tsp
Agave nectar1 tsp
Ice1 cup

Method:

  1. Whisk the cinnamon and agave into a splash of the cold brew until there are no clumps.
  2. Add the rest of the cold brew and stir.
  3. Pour over a glass of ice.
  4. Top with oat milk and dust a little extra cinnamon on top.

Tipfor a frothy cold foam, shake the oat milk with a pinch of cinnamon in a sealed jar for fifteen seconds, then spoon it over the top.

Recipe 04

Clean Iced Mocha

Why it works: mocha done right. Unsweetened cocoa powder brings the chocolate, stevia brings the sweet with zero sugar, and a splash of milk makes it feel indulgent. All the flavor of a mocha, none of the chocolate syrup.

Approx. per glass: 60 cal · 2g sugar

Thermos friendly: yes

IngredientAmount
Strong brewed coffee, chilled¾ cup
Unsweetened cocoa powder1 tsp
Stevia (liquid or powder), to tastea few drops or 1 packet
Milk of choice¼ cup
Ice1 cup

Method:

  1. Make a paste with the cocoa, stevia, and a tablespoon of warm coffee. This stops the cocoa from clumping.
  2. Stir the paste into the rest of the chilled coffee.
  3. Pour over ice.
  4. Top with milk and stir.

Tipa pinch of salt makes cocoa taste more like chocolate. It sounds odd, it works.

Recipe 05

Coconut Cream Cold Foam Cold Brew

Why it works: the special-occasion one that is still clean. A honey-sweetened coconut cold foam floats on top of straight cold brew, so every sip pulls creamy foam and bold coffee together. No whipped cream, no sugar dome.

Approx. per glass: 110 cal · 7g sugar

Thermos friendly: the cold brew yes, add the foam fresh when you arrive

IngredientAmount
Cold brew coffee1 cup
Coconut cream (the thick top of a chilled can)3 tbsp
Honey1 tsp
Ice1 cup

Method:

  1. Whisk or shake the coconut cream with the honey until it thickens into a soft foam, about thirty seconds.
  2. Fill a glass with ice and pour in the cold brew.
  3. Spoon the coconut foam over the top so it floats.
  4. Sip through the foam, do not stir.

Tipchill the can of coconut milk overnight so the cream separates cleanly from the water. Only the thick top layer whips.

Recipe 06

Functional Iced Coffee

Why it works: this is the one you actually pack. It comes in single-serve sticks, so there is no brewing and nothing to spill in your bag. On top of real Arabica coffee energy it carries L-Theanine, L-Tyrosine and GABA, so it is doing more than just waking you up. Stir it, ice it, go.

Approx. per glass: about 25 cal · 230mg caffeine · no added sugar

Thermos friendly: yes. Make it, then pour it into your thermos over ice.

IngredientAmount
Functional coffee stick (our pick below)1 stick
Cold water6 oz
Cold milk of choice¼ cup
Ice1 cup
Stevia, optionalto taste

Method:

  1. Empty one stick into your glass or shaker cup.
  2. Add the cold water and stir until it fully dissolves. It mixes into hot or cold, so no brewing and no blooming.
  3. Fill with ice, then top with your cold milk of choice.
  4. Stir once. Add a drop of stevia only if you want it sweeter. Most people do not.

Tipthis is the recipe to keep a box of in your car, your gym bag, and your beach tote. Recipe 07

Honey Lavender Iced Latte

Why it works: a little floral, a little fancy, still totally clean. Dried culinary lavender steeped into the coffee gives it a calming, coffee-shop feel, honey sweetens it, and almond milk keeps it light. The prettiest drink in the guide.

Approx. per glass: 75 cal · 6g sugar

Thermos friendly: yes

IngredientAmount
Strong brewed coffee, chilled¾ cup
Dried culinary lavender¼ tsp
Honey1 tsp
Unsweetened almond milk¼ cup
Ice1 cup

Method:

  1. Warm the honey with a tablespoon of coffee and the lavender for a minute, then let it steep five minutes and strain out the buds. This makes a quick lavender honey.
  2. Stir the lavender honey into the rest of the chilled coffee.
  3. Pour over ice.
  4. Top with almond milk.

Tipuse culinary lavender only, and go light. A little tastes like a garden, too much tastes like soap.

Recipe 08

Golden Turmeric Coffee

Why it works: the wellness sipper. Turmeric brings an earthy warmth and its well-known anti-inflammatory reputation, black pepper helps your body actually use it, coconut milk makes it golden and creamy, and agave rounds it off. Coffee that feels like a tonic.

Approx. per glass: 90 cal · 6g sugar

Thermos friendly: yes

IngredientAmount
Strong brewed coffee, chilled¾ cup
Ground turmeric¼ tsp
Tiny pinch black pepperpinch
Unsweetened coconut milk¼ cup
Agave nectar1 tsp
Ice1 cup

Method:

  1. Whisk the turmeric, pepper, and agave into a splash of coffee until smooth.
  2. Stir into the rest of the chilled coffee.
  3. Pour over ice.
  4. Top with coconut milk and stir until it turns golden.

Tipturmeric stains, so mix it in a glass you do not mind and rinse right away.

Bonus DIY

Make Your Own Almond Milk

Half these recipes call for almond milk, and the unsweetened store cartons often still carry gums and additives you do not need. Homemade almond milk is three ingredients and about five minutes of hands-on time. It is cleaner, creamier, and cheaper.

Makes about 4 cups

IngredientAmount
Raw almonds1 cup
Filtered water (for blending)4 cups
Water (for soaking)enough to cover
Pinch of saltoptional
Honey, agave, or a dateoptional, to sweeten

Method:

  1. Soak. Cover the almonds with water and let them sit overnight, or at least four hours. They will plump and soften.
  2. Drain and rinse. Pour off the soaking water and give the almonds a rinse.
  3. Blend. Add the soaked almonds and the 4 cups of filtered water to a blender. Blend on high for about a minute until the water turns milky white.
  4. Strain. Pour the mixture through a cheesecloth (or a nut-milk bag) set over a bowl. Gather the cloth and squeeze until only dry pulp is left.
  5. Sweeten, optional. Stir in a little honey, agave, or a pitted date blended back in if you want it sweet. Add a pinch of salt to round it out.
  6. Store. Keep it in a sealed jar in the fridge and use within four days. Shake before pouring, it separates naturally, which is how you know there are no gums holding it together.

Tipdo not throw out the pulp. Spread it on a tray, dry it low in the oven, and you have almond meal for baking.
