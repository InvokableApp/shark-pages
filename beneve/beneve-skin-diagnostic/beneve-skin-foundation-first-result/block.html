Ten free habits, and you only start with one

Don't change everything at once. Pick one thing to work on this week, and let the rest of the list come in behind it while that one settles.

  1. Week 1

    Pick one thing to change: sleep, alcohol, sun or stress

    Choose the one you could still be doing at Christmas, not the one you feel you should choose. One change you keep for ninety days does more than four you drop in the second week.

    • If it's sleep. Same bedtime seven nights a week, weekends included, and the phone out of the bedroom.
    • If it's alcohol. Two nights off to start, not none. Pick which two now.
    • If it's sun. Sunscreen every morning, and a hat when you're outside in the middle of the day.
    • If it's stress. Ten minutes to yourself at the same time every day, on the calendar like anything else.
    • Then leave it alone for four weeks. Write it on the fridge and don't add a second one until it's boring.
  2. Every day

    Mineral sunscreen, even on the grey days

    Uneven tone and a dull cast are the two things sun reliably produces, and they're the two you described. It's the highest return thirty seconds in the whole plan.

    • Check the back of the bottle. Zinc oxide or titanium dioxide, SPF 30 or higher. Those are the mineral filters.
    • How much. About half a teaspoon for your face and neck, which is more than most of us use.
    • Where it goes in the routine. Last step of the morning, after moisturizer and before makeup.
    • Don't stop at your jaw. Neck, chest and the backs of your hands show it first and are the hardest to put right later.
  3. Most days

    Broccoli, cabbage or cauliflower most days

    This is the food group DIM comes from, and it's the one most of us skip. It doesn't have to be virtuous to count.

    • The list. Broccoli, cabbage, Brussels sprouts, kale, cauliflower, arugula.
    • How much. About a cupful, most days.
    • Roasted in olive oil with salt counts. So does frozen. A bag of frozen broccoli and five minutes in a pan is the whole job.
  4. Every day

    Put the color on your plate

    Berries, leafy greens, peppers, tomatoes, anything deeply colored. Antioxidants from food arrive with everything else the plant had in it, and no capsule copies that.

    • Three colors a day. It's a check, not a diet. Green, red and purple across the day is plenty.
    • Frozen berries are cheaper and often better. They're picked riper than the fresh ones that had to travel.
    • Herbs count. Parsley, cilantro and arugula are some of the densest things in the store, and they cost pennies.
  5. Weeks 1 to 3

    Take ultra processed food out for three weeks

    Ultra processed means food built in a factory, not cooked in a kitchen. You're changing what you buy, not buying more, and three weeks is short enough to finish and long enough to see something.

    • Swap the oils. Butter, olive oil or coconut oil in place of sunflower, canola, soybean and anything sold as vegetable oil.
    • Read one line on the packet. If the ingredients list is longer than the food, put it back on the shelf.
    • Keep the list short. Meat, fish, eggs, vegetables, fruit, rice, potatoes, butter, olive oil. All fine, and they always were.
    • Then add things back one at a time. The week you put something back teaches you more than the three weeks without it.
    • What women mention first. Less puffiness around the eyes in the morning, usually somewhere in the second week.
  6. Ten minutes daily

    Ten minutes a day with your face relaxed

    Most of us hold tension all day in the exact places we're trying to smooth: the jaw, the brow, the space between the eyes. Ten minutes of sitting still with all of it loose is free, and it's the only thing on this page that gets easier the longer you do it.

    • How to sit. Jaw unclenched, teeth apart, brow soft, tongue off the roof of your mouth, shoulders down.
    • What to do. Breathe in through your nose for four, out for six. When your mind wanders, come back to your jaw.
    • When. The same ten minutes every day beats a longer sit you have to find time for. First coffee, lunch break, or the car before you go in.
    • Then check in during the day. Set one alarm for the afternoon. Whatever your face is doing when it goes off is what it's been doing for hours.
  7. Weeks 1 to 4

    Two alcohol free nights a week

    Two, not none. Alcohol costs you sleep quality and hydration on the same night, and those are the two things your face reads most directly.

    • Pick the two nights now. Put them on the calendar. Deciding at six o'clock has never worked for anyone.
    • Have the replacement in the fridge. Soda, lime and bitters in a proper glass does most of the job.
    • Watch the morning after the nights you do drink. That's your evidence, and it'll convince you faster than this page will.
  8. Every morning

    Ten minutes of daylight before screens

    Morning light sets the clock that decides when you get tired at night, and that decides the sleep your face runs on.

    • Ten minutes outside within an hour of waking. Standing on the step with your coffee counts.
    • Through a window barely works. Outside on a grey day is many times brighter than the brightest room in your house.
    • Sunglasses off for the first few minutes. The light has to reach your eyes to set the clock.
  9. Week 2

    Then get them out of the rest of the house

    Your face spends eight hours a night on your pillowcase and all day in the air you scented, which is more contact time than anything you put on it on purpose. This is the step people skip, and it's why step one sometimes looks like it didn't work.

    • Laundry first. Unscented detergent, no fabric softener, no dryer sheets. Softener is designed to leave a coating behind, and that coating spends the night on your cheek.
    • Then the air. Candles, plug-ins, room sprays and diffusers all land on your skin, they just take a slower route.
    • Cleaning products. Swap the scented bathroom spray for something unscented, and wear gloves for anything with bleach in it.
    • While you're testing, wash the pillowcase twice a week. It's the single piece of fabric with the most contact time.
  10. Today, then day 90

    Take the photo today

    You can't see a twelve week change in a mirror you look into every morning. Without a photo from day one, you'll decide nothing happened when something did.

    • Same window, same time of day. Daylight from the side, not the overhead bathroom light, which flattens everything.
    • No makeup, no filter, hair back. One straight on, one from each side.
    • Put a reminder in your phone for day 90. Then leave the first photo alone until it goes off.

What not to spend money on

Here's what we'd leave on the shelf, and why.

  • Juice cleanses and detox teasYour liver and kidneys already do that job, and they were installed for free. The teas that produce a visible result do it with a laxative, and what you lose is the water you needed.
  • Brightening actives on irritated skinIf your barrier is already struggling, a brightening acid makes the dullness worse before it makes it better. Get the routine simple first.
  • A dermaroller, or anything that breaks the skin at homeThe risk at home is nothing like the risk in a clinic, and what you described was never a texture problem to begin with.
