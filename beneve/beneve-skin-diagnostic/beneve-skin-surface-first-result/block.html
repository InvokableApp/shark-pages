Start here, and start free

Nine free habits, starting with what you take off your face

None of these cost anything, and most of them are about taking something away. Together they do more for your skin in twelve weeks than a product does on its own.

  1. Week 1

    Get the harsh ingredients off your face

    So much of what gets called sensitive skin is a reaction to something already in the routine. The only way to find out is to take the suspects away for three weeks, then bring them back one at a time.

    • What you're looking for on the back of the bottle. Fragrance or parfum, sulfates (sodium lauryl or laureth sulphate), and alcohol denat high up in the list.
    • Don't throw anything out. Put it in a drawer for three weeks. If your skin doesn't change, you've lost nothing and you can stop wondering about it.
    • Start with what sits on your face longest. Your moisturizer and serum are on all day. A cleanser is on for thirty seconds, so it's the least likely culprit.
    • Add them back one at a time, a week apart. Bring all of them back on the same day and you've learned nothing.
  2. Every day

    Cut your routine down to three things

    A short routine on calm skin beats a long routine on irritated skin, and it costs less. Give it three weeks before you add anything back.

    • Morning. A gentle cleanser or a plain water rinse, a moisturizer, then sunscreen.
    • Night. Cleanser, then the same moisturizer while your face is still damp.
    • That's the lot. No toner, no essence, no acid, no second serum, not for three weeks.
    • Then add one thing at a time, two weeks apart. Add three at once and you'll never know which one helped and which one stung.
  3. Every day

    Wash in water that isn't hot

    Hot water strips the oil your skin barrier is built from, which is why your face feels tight after a shower and looks worse by the evening.

    • Lukewarm at the sink. In the shower, keep your face out of the direct spray and wash it last.
    • Pat dry, don't rub. A clean towel, and a different one from the one that does your hands.
    • Moisturize while your skin is still damp. You're holding water in, and that's easier than putting it back.
  4. Every morning

    Mineral sunscreen, every single morning

    Nothing else on this list does as much for tone and texture. Sun is the biggest driver of the change you described, and window glass doesn't stop all of it, so grey days and desk days count.

    • Check the back of the bottle. Zinc oxide or titanium dioxide, SPF 30 or higher. Those are the mineral filters.
    • How much. About half a teaspoon for your face and neck, which is more than most of us use.
    • Where it goes in the routine. Last step of the morning, after moisturizer and before makeup.
    • Don't stop at your jaw. Neck, chest and the backs of your hands show it first and are the hardest to put right later.
  5. Weeks 1 to 3

    Take ultra processed food out for three weeks

    Ultra processed means food built in a factory, not cooked in a kitchen. You're changing what you buy, not buying more, and three weeks is short enough to finish and long enough to see something.

    • Swap the oils. Butter, olive oil or coconut oil in place of sunflower, canola, soybean and anything sold as vegetable oil.
    • Read one line on the packet. If the ingredients list is longer than the food, put it back on the shelf.
    • Keep the list short. Meat, fish, eggs, vegetables, fruit, rice, potatoes, butter, olive oil. All fine, and they always were.
    • Then add things back one at a time. The week you put something back teaches you more than the three weeks without it.
    • What women mention first. Less puffiness around the eyes in the morning, usually somewhere in the second week.
  6. Ten minutes daily

    Ten minutes a day with your face relaxed

    Most of us hold tension all day in the exact places we're trying to smooth: the jaw, the brow, the space between the eyes. Ten minutes of sitting still with all of it loose is free, and it's the only thing on this page that gets easier the longer you do it.

    • How to sit. Jaw unclenched, teeth apart, brow soft, tongue off the roof of your mouth, shoulders down.
    • What to do. Breathe in through your nose for four, out for six. When your mind wanders, come back to your jaw.
    • When. The same ten minutes every day beats a longer sit you have to find time for. First coffee, lunch break, or the car before you go in.
    • Then check in during the day. Set one alarm for the afternoon. Whatever your face is doing when it goes off is what it's been doing for hours.
  7. Every night

    Sleep on clean fabric, off your face

    Whatever's in your detergent spends eight hours pressed against your cheek, and sleeping face down creases skin that's already dry.

    • Unscented wash, and change the pillowcase twice a week. More often if you use hair products overnight.
    • Silk or satin if you can. It creases the skin less than cotton and it pulls at your hair less.
    • Try to sleep on your back. A pillow under each arm makes it much easier to stay there.
  8. Week 2

    Then get them out of the rest of the house

    Your face spends eight hours a night on your pillowcase and all day in the air you scented, which is more contact time than anything you put on it on purpose. This is the step people skip, and it's why step one sometimes looks like it didn't work.

    • Laundry first. Unscented detergent, no fabric softener, no dryer sheets. Softener is designed to leave a coating behind, and that coating spends the night on your cheek.
    • Then the air. Candles, plug-ins, room sprays and diffusers all land on your skin, they just take a slower route.
    • Cleaning products. Swap the scented bathroom spray for something unscented, and wear gloves for anything with bleach in it.
    • While you're testing, wash the pillowcase twice a week. It's the single piece of fabric with the most contact time.
  9. Today, then day 90

    Take the photo today

    You can't see a twelve week change in a mirror you look into every morning. Without a photo from day one, you'll decide nothing happened when something did.

    • Same window, same time of day. Daylight from the side, not the overhead bathroom light, which flattens everything.
    • No makeup, no filter, hair back. One straight on, one from each side.
    • Put a reminder in your phone for day 90. Then leave the first photo alone until it goes off.

What not to spend money on

Here's what we'd leave on the shelf, and why.

  • Anything promising results in a weekSkin takes about a month to turn over, and collagen takes seasons. A seven day claim is a claim about water.
  • A second expensive eye creamThe skin around your eyes is thinner, it isn't a different organ. While the rest of your routine is one cleanser, an eye cream is the most expensive way to change very little.
  • Micro-current and toning devices, for nowThey aren't nothing, they're the last five percent. Buying one before the basics are in place is buying the roof before the walls.
