The high-protein breakfast and recipe guide

Start with breakfast

Why Protein Is The Piece Everyone Misses

Most breakfasts are built around toast, cereal or a granola bar, and most of them are light on protein.

Protein helps you hold on to the muscle you already have, and it keeps you full for hours. Start the day with about 30g of it and you're not thinking about food till lunch.

These 21 recipes are built on one rule: hit your protein first, every meal, without a chef's skill set or a specialty store. The seven breakfasts each land at about 30g. Pick the serving column that matches your table, follow the list, and you are done.

Three quick habits that make this work

  1. Anchor every meal with a protein you can see (chicken, eggs, fish, beef, beans, Greek yogurt).
  2. Aim for a palm to a palm and a half of protein per person, per meal.
  3. Drink water with every meal. A quiet appetite can also be a quiet thirst.

About the numbers: the calorie and macro figures in this guide are rough estimates to help you plan, not exact measurements. They will shift with the brands you buy, how you trim your meat, and how heavy your hand is with the oil. Use them as a guide, not a scale.

How to read each recipe

  • The ingredient table has three columns. Serves 2, Serves 4, and Serves 6+ (cooking for a crowd). Just follow your column.
  • Macros listed are per serving and stay the same no matter which column you cook.
  • Each recipe ends with a shopping list grouped by aisle so one pass through the store covers it.

Bonus

Cook Once, Eat All Week

The fastest way to hit your protein on a busy day is to have it already cooked. Spend 45 minutes on a Sunday and you turn these recipes into five-minute meals.

Batch these three and you are set

  1. A tray of chicken. Season and roast 2 to 3 pounds of chicken breast or thighs at 425°F for about 25 minutes. Slice some, dice some. Feeds the quinoa bowl, the wraps, and the sheet-pan night.
  2. A pot of protein. Make a double batch of the lean beef chili or the turkey meatballs. Both reheat better than fresh and freeze well.
  3. A dozen hard-boiled eggs and a tub of egg muffins. Grab-and-go breakfast and the egg salad lunch, no morning cooking.

How long it keeps

  • Cooked chicken, beef, turkey, pork: 3 to 4 days in the fridge, 2 to 3 months frozen.
  • Cooked shrimp and salmon: 2 to 3 days in the fridge. Best fresh, so cook these to order.
  • Hard-boiled eggs (in the shell): up to 1 week in the fridge.
  • Chili, meatballs, stuffed peppers: 4 days in the fridge, 3 months frozen.
  • Cooked rice and quinoa: 4 to 5 days in the fridge.

Three rules that keep it safe and tasty

  • Cool food before it goes in the fridge, but get it in within two hours.
  • Store proteins and grains in separate containers so you can mix and match.
  • Reheat once. Take out only what you will eat.

The habit that changes everything: every time you cook a protein, cook double. The second half is tomorrow's lunch you did not have to think about.

Make It Stick

You do not need all 21 at once. Pick three breakfasts, three lunches, and three dinners you actually like, and rotate them this week. Familiar beats perfect. The meal you will repeat is worth more than the one you cook once.

The only rule that matters: protein first, every meal. Build the plate around it and the rest falls into place.
