The Lunchbox Guide

Packable Lunches Built on Protein and Fiber

Ten lunches you pack the night before. Each one fits a single container, holds cold, and needs no microwave.

Start here

Why Lunch

Almost nobody has trouble believing vegetables and protein are good for them. They have trouble eating them on a Tuesday. Dinner is where vegetables go to be planned and then not cooked. Breakfast is usually a rush.

Lunch is different. Lunch is the one meal you can decide about once, on a Sunday, and then carry with you.

A lunch that is mostly carbs lifts you and drops you an hour later. Every lunch in here is built around protein and fiber, so the afternoon holds. Most take under fifteen minutes, and every one says how many it serves.

If you took my Midlife Energy Check, keep your five habits going. This guide is the lunch half of them.

The formula

Build the Lunchbox

Every lunch in this guide is the same five slots. Once you see it, you can build your own and stop needing recipes.

1. A vegetable base, about two cups. Shredded cabbage, massaged kale, roasted broccoli, riced cauliflower, shaved sprouts.

2. A protein, a palm-sized portion. Chicken, tuna, eggs, chickpeas, beef, turkey, edamame. This is what keeps you out of the vending machine at three.

3. Something with flavor. Red onion, garlic in the dressing, fresh herbs, a few spears of asparagus.

4. A dressing with fat and acid. Fat carries flavor and keeps you full. Acid keeps everything tasting bright after four hours in a bag. Never skip the acid.

5. Crunch. Toasted almonds, pumpkin seeds, sesame, radish. This is what makes a packed lunch worth opening.

The container rule. Wet on the bottom, sturdy in the middle, crunchy on top, dressing on the side if you are packing more than a day ahead. Shake at noon.

The Crunch Salad

380 cal31g protein18g carbs21g fat

Red cabbage and kale with shredded chicken and a sesame-ginger dressing. It's the best-holding lunch in this guide, and better on day two.

IngredientAmount
Red cabbage, shredded thin4 cups
Kale, stems out, sliced thin2 cups
Cooked chicken, shredded1 lb
Carrot, grated1
Green onion, sliced4
Toasted sliced almonds½ cup
Sesame oil2 tbsp
Rice vinegar3 tbsp
Fresh ginger, grated1 tbsp
Garlic, crushed1 clove
Honey1 tsp
Salt and pepperto taste
Method
  1. Whisk the sesame oil, rice vinegar, ginger, garlic, honey, salt and pepper into a dressing.
  2. Toss the cabbage, kale, carrot and green onion with the dressing and use your hands. Squeeze it a little as you go, it softens the kale.
  3. Fold through the chicken.
  4. Divide into four containers and top each with almonds.

Broccoli Egg Salad Jar

320 cal26g protein14g carbs19g fat

Broccoli and eggs in a yogurt dressing, so it tastes like a deli lunch instead of a health project.

IngredientAmount
Broccoli florets, chopped small5 cups
Eggs, hard boiled and chopped8
Plain Greek yogurt¾ cup
Dijon mustard1 tbsp
Red onion, diced fine½
Lemon, juice1
Fresh dill, chopped2 tbsp
Pumpkin seeds¼ cup
Salt and pepperto taste
Method
  1. Blanch the broccoli 90 seconds in boiling water, then straight into cold water. Drain it properly and pat it dry.
  2. Mix the yogurt, mustard, lemon, dill, salt and pepper.
  3. Fold in the broccoli, eggs and red onion.
  4. Divide into containers and top with pumpkin seeds.
Lunch 3 of 10 · Serves 4

Kale Caesar with Roasted Chickpeas

410 cal19g protein34g carbs23g fat

Kale is the only salad green that survives a Caesar dressing in a bag until noon, and the roasted chickpeas stay crunchy.

IngredientAmount
Kale, stems out, torn8 cups
Chickpeas, drained and dried2 cans
Olive oil3 tbsp
Smoked paprika1 tsp
Plain Greek yogurt½ cup
Parmesan, grated⅓ cup
Garlic, crushed2 cloves
Lemon, juice1
Anchovy paste, optional1 tsp
Salt and pepperto taste
Method
  1. Heat the oven to 425F. Toss the chickpeas with 1 tbsp of the oil, the paprika and salt, and roast 25 minutes until they rattle.
  2. Whisk the yogurt, parmesan, garlic, lemon, anchovy if using, the rest of the oil, salt and pepper.
  3. Massage the dressing into the kale with your hands for about a minute. It will darken and soften.
  4. Divide into containers, chickpeas on top.

Shaved Brussels and Apple Slaw

340 cal24g protein22g carbs18g fat

Raw Brussels sprouts are sweet and nutty, nothing like the boiled memory. Apple and walnut make it taste like fall.

IngredientAmount
Brussels sprouts, shaved thin6 cups
Apple, crisp, matchsticked1
Cooked turkey or chicken, diced1 lb
Walnuts, toasted and chopped½ cup
Shallot, sliced paper thin1
Olive oil3 tbsp
Apple cider vinegar2 tbsp
Dijon mustard1 tsp
Honey1 tsp
Salt and pepperto taste
Method
  1. Shave the sprouts with a knife, a mandoline, or the slicing disc of a food processor.
  2. Whisk the oil, vinegar, mustard, honey, salt and pepper.
  3. Toss everything together.
  4. Let it sit twenty minutes before you pack it. The sprouts soften just enough.

Sesame Broccoli Slaw Noodle Box

420 cal22g protein48g carbs16g fat

Broccoli slaw is shredded broccoli stalk, the part most people throw away. Cold noodles make it feel like takeout.

IngredientAmount
Broccoli slaw4 cups
Soba or rice noodles, cooked and cooled8 oz dry
Edamame, shelled1½ cups
Red cabbage, shredded2 cups
Green onion, sliced4
Cilantro, chopped½ cup
Sesame oil2 tbsp
Soy sauce or tamari3 tbsp
Rice vinegar2 tbsp
Garlic, crushed2 cloves
Sesame seeds2 tbsp
Chili crisp, optionalto taste
Method
  1. Cook the noodles, drain, and rinse under cold water until they are properly cold. This stops them clumping.
  2. Whisk the sesame oil, soy, vinegar and garlic.
  3. Toss the noodles, broccoli slaw, cabbage, edamame, green onion and cilantro with the dressing.
  4. Divide into containers, sesame seeds on top.
Lunch 6 of 10 · Serves 4

Cauliflower Rice Burrito Bowl

390 cal18g protein36g carbs21g fat

Riced cauliflower doesn't turn to paste in a container the way rice can. Black beans and avocado do the rest.

IngredientAmount
Cauliflower rice6 cups
Black beans, drained2 cans
Red onion, diced1
Garlic, crushed3 cloves
Cumin and smoked paprika1 tsp each
Olive oil2 tbsp
Lime, juice2
Cherry tomatoes, halved2 cups
Avocado2
Cilantro, chopped½ cup
Salt and pepperto taste
Method
  1. Warm the oil in a large pan. Cook the onion and garlic three minutes, then add the cumin and paprika and stir for thirty seconds.
  2. Add the cauliflower rice and cook five minutes, stirring, until it is dry. Wet cauliflower rice turns to mush in a container.
  3. Off the heat, fold in the beans, lime juice, salt and pepper. Let it cool completely.
  4. Pack with tomatoes and cilantro on top. Add avocado in the morning, or cut it in at your desk.

Lunch 8 of 10 · Serves 4

Garlic Beef and Bok Choy Cold Noodles

450 cal34g protein40g carbs18g fat

Bok choy stays crisp in a way most greens don't, and this is the one on the list that satisfies a proper appetite.

IngredientAmount
Flank or sirloin steak, sliced thin1 lb
Bok choy, chopped6 cups
Rice noodles, cooked and cooled8 oz dry
Garlic, crushed4 cloves
Fresh ginger, grated1 tbsp
Soy sauce or tamari3 tbsp
Sesame oil1 tbsp
Rice vinegar2 tbsp
Green onion, sliced4
Sesame seeds2 tbsp
Neutral oil, for the pan1 tbsp
Method
  1. Get a pan properly hot. Sear the beef in one layer, two minutes, and set it aside. Do it in two batches if the pan is crowded.
  2. Same pan, add the bok choy stems for a minute, then the leaves and half the garlic for another minute. You want them bright and still crunchy.
  3. Whisk the soy, sesame oil, vinegar, ginger and the rest of the garlic, raw.
  4. Toss everything with the noodles and the dressing. Cool, then pack with green onion and sesame on top.
Lunch 9 of 10 · Serves 4

Roasted Broccoli and Feta Grain Bowl

430 cal17g protein44g carbs22g fat

Roasting broccoli at high heat makes it something you want to eat cold, which is the whole problem with vegetables and lunch. Feta and lemon do the heavy lifting.

IngredientAmount
Broccoli florets8 cups
Red onion, wedged1
Farro or quinoa, cooked3 cups
Feta, crumbled1 cup
Olive oil4 tbsp
Lemon, zest and juice1
Garlic, crushed2 cloves
Pumpkin seeds½ cup
Chili flakes½ tsp
Salt and pepperto taste
Method
  1. Heat the oven to 450F. Toss the broccoli and onion with 2 tbsp oil, salt and pepper, and roast 20 minutes until the edges char.
  2. Whisk the rest of the oil with the lemon, garlic and chili flakes.
  3. Toss the warm broccoli straight into the dressing. Warm vegetables drink it up.
  4. Fold through the grain, cool, and pack with feta and pumpkin seeds on top.

Green Goddess Cabbage Wraps

360 cal28g protein16g carbs22g fat

The cabbage leaf is the wrap. The dressing is built on garlic, herbs and avocado.

IngredientAmount
Savoy or green cabbage, large outer leaves12
Cooked turkey or chicken, sliced1 lb
Avocado2
Plain Greek yogurt½ cup
Garlic1 clove
Fresh basil and parsley1 cup packed
Lemon, juice1
Cucumber, matchsticked1
Carrot, grated1
Broccoli sprouts2 cups
Salt and pepperto taste
Method
  1. Blanch the cabbage leaves 60 seconds so they roll without cracking, then cold water and pat dry.
  2. Blend one avocado with the yogurt, garlic, herbs, lemon, salt and pepper into a green dressing.
  3. Lay out a leaf, spread the dressing, then turkey, cucumber, carrot, slices of the second avocado, and a pinch of broccoli sprouts.
  4. Roll like a burrito, tucking the sides. Two per person.
Sunday prep

Three Pans, Five Lunches

Forty minutes on a Sunday and most of the week is done. You are not cooking five lunches, you are cooking five components.

Put the oven on at 450F and roast, on two sheet pans: a large head of broccoli in florets, a bunch of asparagus, and a can of drained chickpeas. Twenty to twenty five minutes. This covers lunches 3, 9, and half of anything else.

On the stove, meanwhile: hard boil eight eggs, and cook a batch of farro, quinoa or noodles. Rinse the grains or noodles cold.

On the board, no cooking: shred half a red cabbage and a bag of Brussels sprouts. Both keep three days shredded, in a container, with a paper towel on top.

Make one jar of dressing. Olive oil, lemon, crushed garlic, mustard, salt. It fits nine of the ten lunches here and it takes ninety seconds.

Then all week, assemble. Vegetable base, protein, flavor, dressing, crunch. Two minutes in the morning, or two minutes the night before if mornings are not your friend.

The Grocery List

Vegetables: broccoli, red cabbage, savoy cabbage, kale, Brussels sprouts, cauliflower or cauliflower rice, bok choy, arugula, watercress, radishes, broccoli slaw, asparagus, spinach, avocado

Proteins: chicken, turkey, flank steak, tuna in olive oil, eggs, chickpeas, black beans, edamame, Greek yogurt, feta, parmesan

Flavor: garlic, red onion, shallots, leeks

Grains and noodles: farro, quinoa, soba noodles, rice noodles

Crunch: sliced almonds, walnuts, pumpkin seeds, sesame seeds

Pantry: olive oil, sesame oil, rice vinegar, apple cider vinegar, Dijon mustard, soy sauce or tamari, honey, capers, smoked paprika, cumin, chili flakes

Fresh herbs: dill, cilantro, basil, parsley

The short version, if you buy nothing else: a bag of shredded red cabbage, a dozen eggs, a head of garlic, a lemon, and a bottle of olive oil. That is four of these lunches.

Make it stick

Two You Like, On Repeat

You do not need all ten of these lunches. You need two you like, on repeat, most weeks.

Variety is what people plan for and consistency is what works. Pick the two that sounded good while you were reading, make them this week, and let the other eight sit there for when you get bored.

And be reasonable with yourself about the days it does not happen. A packed lunch four days out of five is a different week from a packed lunch zero days out of five.

The five-minute version: bagged slaw, a tin of tuna in olive oil, a hard-boiled egg, lemon, olive oil, salt.

Where I'd Start

Pick the two that sounded best, shop for them on the weekend, and pack them the night before. Keep the habits from your Energy Check going alongside them.
