Why it doesn't feel like caffeine

The people in the study couldn't tell either

Researchers gave twelve adults 400 mg of caffeine six hours before bed. They lost about an hour of total sleep a night.

Their own sleep diaries said they slept fine. The difference only showed up on the equipment. So this isn't something you catch by paying closer attention to how you feel.

Drake C, Roehrs T, Shambroom J, Roth T. Caffeine effects on sleep taken 0, 3, or 6 hours before going to bed. J Clin Sleep Med, 2013.

Look at your cutoff time again
 

To be clear of one drink that size by the time you get into bed, you'd have had to finish it that early. Not because caffeine is dangerous, but because it lasts far longer than it feels like it does.

Most people won't stop that early. So moving your afternoon drink an hour earlier was never going to be the whole answer. The question becomes what that afternoon drink is made of, and that one you can change without giving anything up.
