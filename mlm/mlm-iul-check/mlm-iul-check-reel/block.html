IUL, in plain words

  • It's life insurance. It pays your family when you die, and it doesn't expire as long as it's paid for.
  • Part of what you pay builds cash value. That part earns a credit tied to how a stock market index does. Your money isn't in the market itself.
  • There's a cap and a floor. In a good year the credit is capped. In a down year it can be zero, but the policy's costs still come out, so the cash value can go down.
  • You can borrow against it. A loan you don't pay back comes out of what your family gets.
  • It costs more than term. There are fees and charges, and leaving in the first years usually means a surrender charge.

Who it can make sense for, and who it doesn't

It can fit someone who wants cover for life and can keep paying the same amount for many years. If you need cover for a set time, like until the kids are grown or the house is paid off, plain term life usually costs much less. The check below helps you see which side you're on.
