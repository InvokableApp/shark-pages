What you sweat out

Sweat is mostly water and salt, with some potassium and magnesium. Water puts the water back. The rest comes back from food.

These are the drinks a good juice bar makes: whole fruit muddled or blended, fresh herbs, coconut water, a salted rim, served cold in the right glass. Fruit brings the potassium, coconut water and yogurt add more, cocoa and nut butter bring magnesium, and the rim brings the sodium. Every card counts all three.

How to read a card

  • The three numbers are the potassium, sodium and magnesium in the glass, in milligrams. Each one is worked out from USDA FoodData Central, ingredient by ingredient.
  • A drink with a salted rim shows the rim on its own line, because how much of it you drink is up to you.
  • Every recipe makes one glass. Double it for two.

How to salt a rim

Put ⅛ teaspoon of salt on a saucer. Run a lime wedge around half the rim and dip it. Salting half the glass means you choose each sip, and the card tells you what the rim adds: about 290 mg of sodium.

Most drinks also take a dash of salt in the glass, about 155 mg. In a spritz or a smoothie you'll barely taste it.

Want it sweeter? A teaspoon of honey or a drop of stevia. Neither changes the numbers much, and the stevia adds none.

Before you salt anything

If a doctor has told you to watch your sodium or potassium (for blood pressure, your kidneys, or a medication), ask them before adding salt or coconut water to your drinks. These are recipes, not medical advice, and they aren't a replacement for drinking water through the day.

Salted Cherry Lime Spritz

Tart cherries and lime under a salted rim, like a margarita.

320mg potassium
190mg sodium
15mg magnesium

+ up to 290 mg sodium on the rim

You need

  • ½ cup frozen tart cherries, thawed
  • 1 tbsp lime juice (half a lime)
  • 1 tsp honey
  • ½ cup coconut water
  • ½ cup sparkling water
  • a dash of salt
  • ⅛ tsp salt, for the rim
  • Lime wedge, to finish

Make it

  1. Put ⅛ tsp salt on a saucer. Run a lime wedge around half the rim and dip it.
  2. Muddle the cherries, lime juice and honey in the glass until the cherries burst.
  3. Fill with ice, add the coconut water and a dash of salt and stir.
  4. Top with sparkling water and give it one gentle stir.

320 mg of potassium from the coconut water and cherries. 190 mg of sodium, most of it from the dash of salt, and the rim adds up to 290 more.

Make it easy: Frozen tart cherries are in the freezer aisle by the berries. Thaw a bag in the fridge and it's a week of these.

Orange Pomegranate Sunrise

Orange on top, red at the bottom, and the prettiest glass in here.

645mg potassium
190mg sodium
30mg magnesium

You need

  • 1 orange, peeled
  • ½ cup coconut water
  • a dash of salt
  • ¼ cup pomegranate seeds
  • ½ cup sparkling water
  • Orange wheel, to finish

Make it

  1. Drop the pomegranate seeds into the bottom of a tall glass and fill it with ice.
  2. Blend the orange, coconut water and a dash of salt until smooth.
  3. Pour it slowly over the ice and top with sparkling water. Don't stir.

645 mg of potassium, from the whole orange, the pomegranate and the coconut water.

Ginger Lime Mule

The bite of a Moscow mule, made with fresh ginger and coconut water.

335mg potassium
50mg sodium
15mg magnesium

+ up to 290 mg sodium on the rim

You need

  • 1 tsp grated fresh ginger
  • 1 tbsp lime juice (half a lime)
  • 1 tsp honey
  • ¾ cup coconut water
  • ½ cup sparkling water
  • ⅛ tsp salt, for the rim
  • Lime wheel, to finish

Make it

  1. Put ⅛ tsp salt on a saucer. Run a lime wedge around half the rim and dip it.
  2. Stir the ginger, lime juice and honey together in the glass until the honey dissolves.
  3. Fill with ice, add the coconut water and top with sparkling water.

Coconut water brings nine tenths of the 335 mg of potassium. The salted rim brings the sodium.

Make it easy: A copper mug if you have one. Grate the ginger on the small holes of a cheese grater, skin and all.

Mango Chili Lime Cooler

Sweet mango with a chili lime salt rim.

530mg potassium
50mg sodium
25mg magnesium

+ up to 310 mg sodium on the rim

You need

  • ¾ cup mango chunks
  • ¾ cup coconut water
  • 1 tbsp lime juice (half a lime)
  • ⅛ tsp salt, for the rim
  • ¼ tsp chili powder, for the rim
  • Lime wedge, to finish

Make it

  1. Mix the salt and chili powder on a saucer. Run a lime wedge around half the rim and dip it.
  2. Blend the mango, coconut water and lime juice until smooth.
  3. Pour over ice.

Coconut water and mango bring 530 mg of potassium. The chili salt rim adds 310 mg of sodium.

Make it easy: Frozen mango works. Freeze your own fresh chunks so the numbers stay true.

Hibiscus Lime Cooler

Deep red, tart like cranberry, and caffeine-free.

270mg potassium
195mg sodium
15mg magnesium

You need

  • 1 cup hibiscus tea, brewed and cooled
  • ½ cup coconut water
  • 1 tbsp lime juice (half a lime)
  • 1 tsp honey
  • a dash of salt
  • Lime wheel, to finish

Make it

  1. Steep one hibiscus tea bag in a cup of hot water for 5 minutes. Let it cool, or make a jug the night before.
  2. Stir in the coconut water, lime juice, honey and a dash of salt.
  3. Pour over ice.

Most of the 270 mg of potassium is the coconut water. The hibiscus brings the color and the tartness.

Make it easy: Brew four bags in a quart jar and keep it in the fridge for the week.
