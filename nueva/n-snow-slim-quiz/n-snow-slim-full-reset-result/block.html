Your plan

All Three, In Order

Your answers did not concentrate anywhere, which usually means all three are true rather than none of them. That is a common place to be after a few years of doing the right things, and the only real risk is trying to fix everything in the same week.

What your answers pointed at

Three lanes. Almost everyone has one that leads, and the plan below is ordered by yours.

AppetiteFirst

Start here, because it is the fastest to respond and because the other two are hard to read while the evenings are still moving the week around.

CompositionSecond

Second, once the evenings are steady. This is the lane the trouble areas actually sit in.

FirmnessThird

Third, and slowest. It is mostly built rather than lost, so it needs the other two settled to be worth the effort.

The ninety days that cost nothing

One lane at a time, four weeks each. Doing all three at once is the reason the last three attempts are in the drawer, and it is not a discipline failure. It is a design failure.

  1. Week 1

    Pick the first lane and ignore the other two

    Appetite first unless you have a reason to differ. The other two do not go anywhere, and you cannot tell what is working when three things changed on the same Monday.

  2. Day 1, then monthly

    Use a tape, not the scale

    Waist at the navel, hips at the widest, mid thigh, upper arm. The scale cannot tell you where anything went, which is exactly the question you came here with.

  3. Weeks 1 to 4

    Protein early, fiber with lunch

    The two changes that do the most for the first lane, and they cost you nothing except deciding once. Everything else in the appetite lane is downstream of these two.

  4. Weeks 5 to 8

    Eat at maintenance, and add walking

    The second lane. It will feel like the wrong direction and it is the change most likely to have been missing from the last two years.

  5. Weeks 9 to 12

    Resistance training, and progress the load

    The third lane. Fewer reps, more weight, longer rests, and progress it. Classes and circuits maintain what is there; they do not build.

  6. Every day, all twelve weeks

    Seven hours of sleep

    The one thing that belongs in all three lanes, which is why it is here rather than in one of them.

  7. Day 1, then day 90

    Take the photo today

    Same light, same time of day, same clothes, from the front and the side. You cannot see a twelve week change in a mirror you look into every morning, and without the photo you will conclude nothing worked when something did.

What not to spend money on

Being told what to skip is worth more than being told what to buy, and it is the part almost nobody puts on a page like this one.

  • Doing all three at onceIt is the reason the last three attempts did not stick. Twelve weeks of one thing at a time beats four weeks of everything, and you have already run the second experiment.
  • Another cutTwo of your three lanes are made worse by a deficit. Whatever the first lane turns out to be, it is almost certainly not eating less again.
  • A new plan every time one stallsA lane that has had four honest weeks has told you something. A lane that had nine days has not.
