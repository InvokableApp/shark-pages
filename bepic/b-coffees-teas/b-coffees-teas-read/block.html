Metabolism Coffees and Teas

6 warm blended coffees and 6 warm teas, made with the coffee or tea you already drink

  • 6 coffees
  • 6 teas
  • Under 10 minutes
  • No new gear
12Recipes

Your morning cup can do more

You already drink coffee or tea every morning. The caffeine in it can give your metabolism a lift, which makes it one of the easiest habits to build on, because you don't have to start it.

These recipes add the spices and extras people reach for when they want their morning cup working with them: cinnamon, ginger, cacao, a little coconut oil, green tea, matcha.

They keep the extras light. Butter-and-oil coffees can run to several hundred calories a cup. Here it's a teaspoon of oil at most, and most of the cups have none.

Brew it your way

  1. Drip, French press, pods, cold brew or instant all work. Use about 1 cup (8 oz) of the coffee you already make.
  2. For tea, a bag or a teaspoon of loose leaf per cup. Green tea likes water just off the boil (about 175°F, or boiled and left a minute); black tea and herbal tea take it boiling.
  3. "Blend" means 15 to 20 seconds with a stick blender, a milk frother or a lidded blender (vent the lid, hot drinks push it off). A jar with a tight lid, shaken hard with a towel over it, works too.
  4. Any milk works: dairy, oat, almond, coconut or none.

Before you start

Most healthy adults are fine with up to 400 mg of caffeine a day from everything combined (the FDA's general guide). A cup of brewed coffee has roughly 80 to 100 mg, green tea about 30 to 50.

Keep your last caffeinated cup at least 6 hours before bed.

Check with your doctor first if you're pregnant, nursing, under 18, have a medical condition or take medication.

Cinnamon Spice Coffee

Cinnamon is a favourite in metabolism drinks, and it brings sweetness without the sugar.

Ingredient1 mug2 mugs
Hot brewed coffee1 cup2 cups
Ground cinnamon½ tsp1 tsp
Ground nutmega pincha pinch
Milk of your choice2 tbsp¼ cup
Maple syrup (optional)½ tsp1 tsp

Method

  1. Put the cinnamon and nutmeg in the mug first.
  2. Pour in the hot coffee and the milk, and the maple syrup if you like it sweeter.
  3. Blend 15 to 20 seconds until it's frothy. Blending is what keeps the cinnamon from sitting on top.
  4. Dust a little more cinnamon on the foam.

Tip

Stir a teaspoon of cinnamon into the grounds before you brew instead. It flavours the whole pot.

Coconut Oil Blended Coffee

A spoon of coconut oil blended in makes a creamy cup that a lot of people say keeps them going until lunch.

Ingredient1 mug2 mugs
Hot brewed coffee1 cup2 cups
Coconut oil or MCT oil1 tsp2 tsp
Milk of your choice2 tbsp¼ cup
Ground cinnamona pinch¼ tsp

Method

  1. Pour the hot coffee into a blender jar or a tall mug.
  2. Add the oil, the milk and the cinnamon.
  3. Blend 20 seconds, until it's pale and foamy like a latte. Stirred, the oil just floats.

Tip

Start with half a teaspoon of oil if you're new to it, and build up. A teaspoon is plenty.

Cacao Cayenne Mocha

Cayenne is a favourite in metabolism drinks for its heat, and cacao turns it into a mocha without a sugary syrup.

Ingredient1 mug2 mugs
Hot brewed coffee1 cup2 cups
Unsweetened cacao or cocoa powder1 tbsp2 tbsp
Cayenne peppera tiny pincha pinch
Ground cinnamon¼ tsp½ tsp
Honey or maple syrup1 tsp2 tsp
Milk of your choice¼ cup½ cup

Method

  1. Warm the milk in a small pan or the microwave.
  2. Put the cacao, cayenne, cinnamon and honey in the mug and stir in a splash of the hot coffee to make a paste.
  3. Add the rest of the coffee and the warm milk.
  4. Blend 15 to 20 seconds until it's smooth and foamy.

Tip

Cayenne builds as you drink. Start with the smallest pinch and add more tomorrow if you want it hotter.

Pumpkin Spice Coffee

Real pumpkin and real spice instead of a flavoured syrup, so it tastes like fall without the sugar.

Ingredient1 mug2 mugs
Hot brewed coffee1 cup2 cups
Canned pumpkin purée (not pie filling)1 tbsp2 tbsp
Pumpkin pie spice¼ tsp½ tsp
Milk of your choice¼ cup½ cup
Maple syrup1 tsp2 tsp

Method

  1. Warm the milk with the pumpkin, the spice and the maple syrup, whisking until it's smooth and steaming.
  2. Pour in the hot coffee.
  3. Blend 15 to 20 seconds until it's foamy, and dust a little spice on top.

Tip

Freeze the rest of the can in an ice cube tray. One cube is about a tablespoon, ready for tomorrow.

Lemon Ginger Green Tea

Green tea is the tea people reach for most when they want their metabolism working with them, and ginger and lemon wake it right up.

Ingredient1 mug2 mugs
Green tea bag (or 1 tsp loose)12
Fresh ginger, thinly sliced4 slices8 slices
Water1 cup2 cups
Lemon¼½
Honey (optional)1 tsp2 tsp

Method

  1. Boil the water and pour it over the ginger. Leave it 4 minutes.
  2. Add the tea bag and steep 2 to 3 minutes. Any longer and green tea turns bitter.
  3. Take out the bag, squeeze in the lemon and stir in the honey.

Tip

Leave the ginger in your mug and top it up with hot water after lunch for a second, milder cup.

Cinnamon Spice Chai

Black tea for the caffeine and a pan of warm spices, made from scratch instead of a sugary concentrate.

Ingredient1 mug2 mugs
Black tea bag (or 1 tsp loose)12
Cinnamon stick11
Cardamom pods, cracked35
Whole cloves23
Fresh ginger, sliced2 slices4 slices
Black peppera pincha pinch
Water¾ cup1½ cups
Milk of your choice¼ cup½ cup
Honey or maple syrup1 tsp2 tsp

Method

  1. Put the spices and the water in a small pan and simmer 5 minutes.
  2. Turn off the heat, add the tea and steep 3 minutes.
  3. Add the milk and warm it through without boiling.
  4. Strain into a mug and stir in the honey. Blend it for a foamy top.

Tip

Simmer a big batch of the spices on Sunday, strain, and keep it in the fridge. Each morning, heat a cup with a tea bag and milk.

Coconut Matcha Latte

Matcha is the whole green tea leaf ground to a powder, so you drink the leaf itself, with a creamy coconut finish.

Ingredient1 mug2 mugs
Matcha powder1 tsp2 tsp
Hot water (not boiling)2 tbsp¼ cup
Coconut milk (the carton kind)¾ cup1½ cups
Honey1 tsp2 tsp
Coconut oil (optional)½ tsp1 tsp

Method

  1. Whisk the matcha with the hot water until there are no lumps. A small whisk or a fork works.
  2. Warm the coconut milk until it steams.
  3. Add it to the matcha with the honey and the oil, and blend 15 to 20 seconds.

Tip

Sift the matcha through a tea strainer first and it never clumps.

Hibiscus Cinnamon Tea

The one with no caffeine: tart, bright red and warming, for an afternoon or evening cup.

Ingredient1 mug2 mugs
Dried hibiscus (or 1 hibiscus tea bag)1 tbsp2 tbsp
Cinnamon stick11
Water1 cup2 cups
Lime or lemona wedge2 wedges
Honey (optional)1 tsp2 tsp

Method

  1. Boil the water and pour it over the hibiscus and the cinnamon stick.
  2. Steep 5 to 7 minutes, until it's deep red.
  3. Strain, squeeze in the lime and stir in the honey.

Tip

It's just as good cold. Make a jug, leave it in the fridge, and drink it over ice.

Peppermint Green Tea

Cool mint with green tea's caffeine: a lighter cup for after lunch.

Ingredient1 mug2 mugs
Green tea bag (or 1 tsp loose)12
Peppermint tea bag or 6 fresh mint leaves12
Water, just off the boil1 cup2 cups
Lemon (optional)a slice2 slices

Method

  1. Boil the water and leave it a minute.
  2. Pour it over both tea bags (or the green tea and the mint leaves) and steep 3 minutes.
  3. Take out the bags and add the lemon if you like.

Tip

Crush the fresh mint leaves in your hand before they go in. It releases more flavour.
