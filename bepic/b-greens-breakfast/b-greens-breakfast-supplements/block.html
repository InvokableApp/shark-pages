My Morning Supplement List

What's worth taking in the morning, and what to skip

Food first, then this

You've got the recipe guide, so you already have the part that matters most. Vegetables on a plate bring fiber, water and bulk, and they stay the base of everything here.

This list is for the rest: what people take in the morning, graded one of four ways.

Worth it. Real evidence, in people, at a dose you can buy.

Worth a try. Plausible, with small or mixed studies behind it, and cheap enough that trying it for a month is reasonable.

Thin. Popular, and the evidence doesn't back the claim on the front of the bottle.

Skip. Sold hard for the morning routine, and it doesn't do what it says.

Reading a label in 60 seconds

Three things on a label decide whether you can check it.

A blend tells you the total and nothing else. "Super Greens Blend 5,400 mg" with five plants listed under it means the five weigh 5,400 mg together. The first could be most of it and the last a pinch. If an amount sits beside an ingredient, you can check it. If it's inside a blend, you can't.

More ingredients means less of each. A scoop weighs about 9 to 12 grams. A powder that lists 75 ingredients has room for very little of any one of them.

Look for a third-party test. NSF, Informed Choice and USP check that the bottle holds what the label says. A company that tests its own batches should say who did the testing.

The ones worth it

Protein at breakfast.

Worth it.

Food, not a supplement, and it's the best-supported thing on this list. Around 25 to 30 grams at breakfast keeps you fuller through the morning. Eggs, Greek yogurt and the savory oats in the recipe guide all get you most of the way.

Vitamin D.

Worth it if you're low.

It's common to run low in winter and if you work indoors. Take it with a meal that has some fat in it, because it absorbs better that way. The right dose depends on how low you are, so ask for a blood test first.

Coffee, or caffeine.

Worth it, for alertness.

It's the best-evidenced thing there is for alertness. About 100 to 200 mg (one or two cups) is the useful range. Half of it is still in you five hours later, which is why a morning cup is fine and a 3pm cup costs you sleep. The FDA's general ceiling for healthy adults is 400 mg a day from everything combined.

L-theanine with your coffee.

Worth it.

An amino acid from tea leaves that takes the edge off caffeine and keeps the alertness. Around 100 to 200 mg alongside your coffee. Matcha has some naturally.

Creatine.

Worth it if you lift.

3 to 5 grams a day, any time of day, so the morning is as good as any. The evidence is strongest for strength and muscle when you train. It's one of the most studied supplements there is.

Worth a try

Omega-3 (fish oil).

Worth a try if you don't eat oily fish.

The case is strongest for people who rarely eat salmon, sardines or mackerel. Take it with breakfast to cut the aftertaste.

Probiotics.

Worth a try, and the strain matters.

The evidence is specific to each strain and each problem, so a label that names its strains and its count (CFU) is the one to pick. Give any probiotic about a month before you decide.

Functional mushrooms (lion's mane, reishi, maitake).

Worth a try.

Used for centuries, with the human research still young and growing. The easy way in is a blend you'd take anyway, like a greens drink or a mushroom coffee.

A greens powder.

Worth a try as a top-up.

It's the easiest way to get some greens into a morning that otherwise has none: one scoop in water, no cooking. It works best alongside vegetables rather than instead of them, since a scoop doesn't bring their fiber. Pick one with a short list of named ingredients and a third-party test.

Thin, and skip

A multivitamin.

Thin, unless you're in a group that runs short.

For most people eating a mixed diet, the big trials find little benefit. The exceptions are real: people who eat no animal products, people over sixty, and anyone pregnant or trying, where B12, folate or iron can matter.

An apple cider vinegar shot.

Thin, as a morning shot.

The small effect it has is on the rise after a carb-heavy meal, so a shot on an empty stomach at 7am misses it. It's hard on tooth enamel, so dilute it and don't sip it all morning.

Celery juice.

Skip.

The claims about it aren't backed by any trial, and juicing takes out the fiber, which is the most useful thing in celery.

Lemon water for your liver.

Skip the reason.

A glass of water first thing is a fine habit. The lemon doesn't do a job your liver and kidneys aren't already doing.

Biotin for hair and nails.

Skip, unless you're short of it.

Real deficiency is rare. Worth knowing: the FDA has warned that high-dose biotin can throw off some blood tests, including one used to check for a heart attack. If you take it, tell whoever orders your bloodwork.

A morning, in order

On waking, before anything. If you take thyroid medication, it goes first, with water only, 30 to 60 minutes before coffee or food. Coffee and breakfast both get in its way.

With breakfast. Vitamin D, fish oil and a multivitamin, if you take them. They absorb better with food.

Coffee. In the morning, and done by early afternoon.

Keep apart. Iron absorbs poorly next to coffee, tea or calcium. If you take iron, take it at least an hour away from them.

Mornings you don't cook. A greens drink, if you use one. It's what I do, and the next page is the one I use.

If you only start with one

1. Protein at breakfast. It costs nothing extra and it's the strongest thing on the list.

2. One recipe from the guide, twice a week. The frittata or the burritos, made on Sunday, cover most weekday mornings.

3. Then, if you want, one product for a month. Not a stack. One thing at a time, so you know what did what.
